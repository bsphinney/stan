      IPS: Instrument Performance Score

      IPS places a run's depth against a fixed calibration set for its instrument and SPD: 60 is the median calibration run and 90 its 90th percentile. It weights precursors 50% (PSMs for DDA), peptides 30% and proteins 20%. The calibration set is 352 UC Davis runs from April 2026.

      IPS is not shown on this page yet. A lookup error scores Exploris and timsTOF runs against a pooled all-instrument reference instead of their own, so Exploris scores read too low and timsTOF scores too high. Scores return once they are recomputed.

      The HeLa standard

      Pierce HeLa Protein Digest Standard (Thermo Scientific 88328 / 88329): a tryptic digest of HeLa S3 with more than 15,000 proteins and under 10% missed cleavages. One commercial standard means differences reflect the instrument and LC, not sample prep.

      Points across peak

      How many MS2 scans sample each precursor's elution peak. STAN's guideline: 12 or more for reliable quantitation, 6–12 acceptable depending on peak shape, under 6 and error grows quickly. At high SPD on short columns, cycle time can exceed peak width.

      Background: Matthews & Hayes, Anal. Chem. 1976, 48, 1375–1382, on how few sampling points bias GC-MS ratio measurements. doi:10.1021/ac50003a028

      CHANGED · per instrument

      Mass Accuracy Drift (MS1)

      Median corrected MS1 mass error per run (absolute ppm). Tracks Orbitrap and qTOF calibration stability: a rising line signals lock-mass failure or thermal drift before IDs drop.

      MS1 Signal (TIC proxy)

      Total MS1 ion current per run, a proxy for ion-source health. A drop of more than 2× points at a dirty emitter, low flow or a sample-prep problem before IDs fall. timsTOF reads about 1.5 log units lower because of its detector, not its health: compare an instrument with itself.

      Dynamic Range

      log10(p99 / p01) of precursor intensity. Compresses when the ion source is dirty or the LC is losing pressure. Like MS1 signal it depends on the detector, so compare an instrument with itself.

      Points Across Peak

      Data points per chromatographic peak (after Matthews & Hayes 1976). A rising trend at constant SPD signals column degradation (peaks broadening). Validated against Spectronaut (median 9 on timsTOF 100 SPD).

      Lab trend vs. reference

      One lab's QC runs over time in a single cohort. The gold band is that lab's own baseline, fixed from its first 30 runs in the cohort, so problems show up even when no other lab shares the cohort, and a bad stretch is flagged instead of widening the band. The white line tracks the median of the last 15 runs, so slow drift is visible too. Other labs in the same cohort appear as a grey reference band once there are any.

      Evosep PEG Watch

      Polyethylene glycol (PEG) from tips, solvents and plastics shows up in MS1 as a ladder of ions 44.026 Da apart, and it can cost identifications. STAN measures it in QC runs. Evosep labs that opt in share their per-run PEG here, so you can see whether your level is normal and whether a fix worked. PEG needs no database search, so this board is separate from the benchmark above and the filters at the top of the page do not apply to it.

      Put your lab on the board

      Any lab running STAN 1.2 or later can join. PEG is read straight from raw MS1, so it needs no community search.

      1. Claim your lab name. Run stan community-claim to prove it with an emailed code. Do this first: an unclaimed name can be claimed by anyone, who then takes over its place on the board. Unclaimed names show as unverified.
      2. Opt in. Add peg_share: true to ~/.stan/community.yml. Sharing stays off until you do.
      3. Sync. Run stan peg-sync. It sends every QC run that has a PEG measurement, and the relay keeps only what changed, so it is safe to run again at any time.

      Lab names are pseudonyms. Shared per QC run: date, instrument model, LC system, Evosep method (SPD), acquisition mode, sample type and amount, and the PEG share, score, ion count and class. File and sample names, raw data, spectra and serial numbers never leave your lab.

      Found PEG? Isolate the source in one night with STAN's Evosep PEG diagnostic.

      How the ranking works

      PEG share of MS1. STAN reads 80 MS1 scans spread across the gradient and matches peaks within 5 ppm to the PEG ladder: PEG1–20 as [M+H]+, [M+NH4]+ and [M+Na]+, spaced 44.026 Da (C2H4O). The share is the matched intensity over all MS1 peaks above 104 counts. Unlike the 0–100 PEG score, it keeps rising with contamination, so it still separates labs that all score 100.

      Cohorts. Labs are ranked only against the same instrument family and Evosep method, because detector response and gradient length change the number. Only Evosep runs are ranked.

      Rank. Median over the window's QC runs, lowest first, with at least 5 runs; ties go to more clean runs, then more runs. Clean means a PEG score below 20. Runs with no PEG measurement are left out; they never count as clean.

      Badges. Cleanest is rank 1 once two or more labs are ranked. Most improved is the biggest fall in median against the previous window, if it fell by 15% or more and by at least 0.5 percentage points, also once two or more labs are ranked. Change stays blank when the previous window had fewer than 5 runs or a median below 0.1%, where a percent change is noise.
